# Two-Site Operating Plan

**Status:** Planning-level operating concept; **no depot site identified or controlled** · **Prepared:** 2026-07-27
**External-use status:** internal_only
**Related:** [Program and Scenario Reconciliation](../planning/Program%20and%20Scenario%20Reconciliation.md) · [Electrical Basis of Design](Electrical%20Basis%20of%20Design.md) · [Construction Budget Basis](../cost/Construction%20Budget%20Basis.md)

> **No off-site depot has been identified, optioned, leased, or acquired.** No fleet operator, mobility partner, or technology company has any documented relationship with this project. Fleet sizes, vehicle counts, and depot costs below are planning hypotheses. AV or fleet revenue is excluded from every base case.

## 1. Why two sites

The two-site split is usually argued on cost. On this site it is also **structurally required**, and that is the stronger argument.

**The capacity argument.** Under Basis B, the 6-storey scheme (SA-B) produces 231 structured stalls against a City code requirement of roughly 232 for its own office and med-tail area. It has **no surplus**. There is no deck capacity for a fleet, at any price, unless clinical area is reduced or the City grants a parking reduction. Overnight fleet storage on South Andrews is not something the owner is choosing against — under the leading program it is **not available**.

Even the maximum-fleet scheme (SA-A: 8 storeys, five parking levels, 216,000 GSF) yields only about **140 operational fleet and staging positions** after code parking, secure-zone boundary loss, turnaround and manoeuvring reserve. That is roughly one third of the 250–400 vehicle fleet contemplated, on the most expensive and most entitlement-exposed scheme available.

**The cost argument.**

| Measure | On South Andrews | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Core all-in cost per operational fleet position, SA-A | **$544,977** | The scheme that maximises fleet capacity |
| Core all-in cost per operational fleet position, SA-B | **$3,326,378** | The leading balanced scheme — its 18 ground bays are the *only* positions it has, because the decks carry no surplus |
| Core all-in cost per operational fleet position, SA-C | $1,514,774 | The scheme that minimises capital |
| Structured parking cost per gross stall | $41,818 | Against a WGI 2026 national median of $33,300 |
| Land cost per SF | ~$209 (at the working input) | The depot function would occupy the most expensive square footage in the program |
| Illustrative industrial depot land, 2.5 acres at $40/SF | ~$4,356,000 | **Unverified benchmark.** No Broward industrial land study exists in the repository |

Housing a vehicle in a structured deck on a $209/SF healthcare corner, at more than half a million dollars per usable fleet position, is not a rational allocation of capital under any operating model on record.

**The electrical argument.** With an on-site depot charging 120 vehicles, the managed service basis computes to **3,208 kW**, indicating **medium-voltage (13.2 kV) service** with owner-side transformers and a vault. Without it, SA-B computes to **1,143 kW** — a conventional **480 V secondary service**. The avoided capital is roughly **$2.0M of utility service** plus about **$1.1M of battery** that exists largely to hold the medium-voltage case down. See [Electrical Basis of Design](Electrical%20Basis%20of%20Design.md) §6.

**Total quantified advantage of the two-site strategy: on the order of $3M in avoided electrical capital, plus the entire structured-deck cost of any depot function, plus the entitlement risk of a parking-heavy accessory-use argument.**

### Daily cycling capacity

The right measure for a transfer-and-staging site is not stalls but **throughput**. With 18 ground bays at an average dwell of 20 minutes across a 14-hour operating day, and allowing 25% for queuing inefficiency and peak clustering:

**18 bays × (14 hours ÷ 0.333 hours) × 0.75 ≈ 560 vehicle movements per day.**

This is a planning calculation, not a validated operating figure — it assumes a dwell time no operator has confirmed and a demand pattern no study has established. But the order of magnitude matters: **South Andrews can plausibly cycle several hundred vehicle movements a day through 18 bays.** Its constraint is not throughput. Its constraint is **storage**, and storage is exactly what belongs off-site.

This is the operating case for the two-site model stated positively: South Andrews is a high-throughput, low-storage transfer point serving a healthcare campus. The depot is a low-throughput, high-storage industrial facility. They are different buildings on different land at different prices, and combining them produces the worst of both.

## 5. If the depot is delayed, unavailable, or more expensive

This is the question the strategy must survive, and it must be answered before capital is committed.

| Consequence | Effect | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **No overnight fleet storage anywhere** | Fleet operations cannot scale beyond what 18 ground bays support with same-day cycling | Third-party parking agreements as an interim; contract storage with an existing operator |
| **~$3.15M of SA-B capital becomes stranded** | Mobility circulation and security ($1.25M), edge data infrastructure ($1.25M), EV-ready conduit beyond installed ($50K), and incremental FPL service above a clinical-only requirement ($600K) exist to serve an operation with no back-of-house | **Do not commit any of it before the depot-site gate closes.** This is the single most important sequencing rule in the plan |
| **Pressure to rebuild the depot on South Andrews** | Returns the project to SA-A: medium-voltage service, discretionary height review, a 1.66× accessory-parking argument, $76.3M core cost, and still only ~140 fleet positions | **Reject.** SA-A has already been tested and fails on capacity, entitlement and electrical grounds simultaneously |
| **Depot costs materially more than benchmark** | Two-site advantage narrows | The advantage is large enough to absorb substantial error: ~$3M of avoided electrical capital alone, before land and structure |
| **Mobility programme does not proceed at all** | South Andrews reverts to a clinical and med-tail building with public parking | **This is the scenario the design must survive without penalty.** SA-C is deliberately structured so that Phase 1 stands alone as a healthcare building. Its mobility elements — chargers, transfer canopy, staging bays — total roughly **$1.09M** and are the only genuinely mobility-specific spend |

**Design rule that follows:** every mobility-specific element on South Andrews must be either (a) independently useful to the clinical and retail program, or (b) deferred behind a signed commitment. Charging and the transfer canopy pass test (a) — patients, staff and rideshare use them regardless. The edge data room and the full security and gating package pass neither and should not be built speculatively.

## 6. Sequencing and gates

| Gate | Question | Consequence if it fails |
|---|---|---|
| **Depot-site gate** | Has a specific off-site depot been identified, priced, zoned-verified, and secured under option or LOI? | **Until this closes, build no mobility-specific infrastructure on South Andrews beyond chargers and the transfer canopy** |
| **Operator gate** | Is there a signed master lease, minimum-use guarantee, or capital contribution from a fleet operator? | Until this closes, no AV or fleet revenue enters any pro forma, and no bay is built beyond what clinical drop-off needs |
| **Charging window gate** | Has the operator agreed a charging window of 10–12 hours rather than 8? | An 8-hour window costs roughly 481 kW more depot service than a 12-hour window — negotiate before specifying equipment |
| **Capacity gate** | Has FPL capacity been confirmed at both sites? | Neither site's service size is confirmed by anyone |

## 7. What is not established

- No depot site exists, has been identified, or has been priced against a real listing.
- No fleet operator, AV company, or mobility partner has any documented relationship with this project. Waymo, Zoox, Tesla and Freebee appear in S3 `Sources!A14:A17` expressly as **context only** — "No partnership or site demand assumed."
- No fleet size, utilisation rate, dwell time, or vehicle mix has been validated by anyone.
- The 250–400 vehicle figure is a planning hypothesis with no demand study behind it.
- No industrial land or lease study exists for Broward County.
- The daily-cycling calculation in §3 rests on an assumed dwell time.

**The two-site strategy is the right answer to a question the project has not yet earned the right to ask.** Its logic is sound and quantified; its inputs are hypotheses. It should govern how South Andrews is *designed* — so that nothing is built which presumes an on-site depot — long before it governs any spending on a depot itself.
