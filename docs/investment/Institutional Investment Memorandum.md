## Part I — Executive Layer

## 1. Executive Decision Summary

### 1.1 What this project is

A **proposed** healthcare-anchored, mixed-use institutional development on a 38,207 SF (0.88-acre) three-parcel corner assemblage at 901–917 South Andrews Avenue, Fort Lauderdale, Florida [VF — SITE-01/02, CAN-002]. Under ratified decision D-P2, healthcare is the anchor; structured parking, EV charging, AV staging readiness, solar, battery storage, and data infrastructure are enabling infrastructure or optional value layers, and base-case feasibility must not depend on speculative optional revenue [CAN-010/011/012]. The sponsor does not currently own the property; no site control, contract, or acquisition exists [VF — CAN-003].

### 1.2 Transaction status

The only ratified transaction input is a working opening offer of **$8,000,000** — an acquisition-strategy input only (D-P1); it is not proof of value and not, by itself, authority to submit an offer. Negotiating authority, maximum authorized purchase price, walk-away price, and final offer conditions are unresolved [OQ-20]. A September 2025 third-party appraisal concluded $11,100,000 fee simple — an appraisal opinion, not a verified market-clearing price, and one carrying documented comparable-selection critiques [SD — ACQ-03/09; S1 §4; S8 p.7]. See Chapter 5.

### 1.3 The finding that controls this memorandum

> **Both current financial models independently indicate that the base project, as presently modeled at an $8,000,000 land basis, does not satisfy the modeled institutional return requirements.** [MO — Project Canon §3]

The land basis in that sentence is the D-P1 strategy input (acquisition-strategy input only; not proof of value or authority). Modeled yields on cost are 3.83–4.07% against 6.25–7.0% modeled requirements; Basis A shows development profit of approximately −$27.7M / −$33.9M (6-/8-story) and Basis B shows annual partner/grant/master-lease gaps of approximately $1.89M–$3.23M [MO — CAN-020/021; S2 `Returns!B13:B31`; S3 `Financing & Returns!D21:E28`]. These are planning-level outputs of unaudited models on unadopted program bases [R-20]; they are the current record, and no downstream document may omit or contradict them [Project Canon §8.4].

**Extension of the finding (2026-07-27 integrated development pass).** A derived residual-land analysis — which reproduces S3's published stabilized NOI to within $107 before any analyst judgment is applied — establishes that **the modeled shortfall is not curable by the acquisition price**. At a land basis of zero, no tested scenario exceeds a 4.49% yield on cost; the leading balanced scheme reaches 3.62%. The office rent required to reach a 7.0% yield on the leading scheme is approximately $131/RSF NNN against $50 modeled and $36 in the alternative basis [MO — FIN-06/07/08; R-21]. Every increment of vertical scale earns between 2.12% and 3.43% on the incremental capital. The consequence for this memorandum is that the acquisition question is one of **transaction structure and partner support**, not of negotiated price. Full analysis: [Integrated Development Recommendation](Integrated%20Development%20Recommendation.md).

## 2. Investment Thesis

The ratified thesis (D-P2) is that a healthcare-anchored, mixed-use institutional project — flexible clinical and medical-office space over a convertible structured-parking podium with a medical-retail ground floor — is the strongest defensible use of this site, with mobility and energy systems as enabling infrastructure and optionality rather than the business [CAN-010/011].

**Why the site may merit continued investment [SD/REC]:**

1. **Location fundamentals (verified).** Downtown proximity, airport proximity, hospital-corridor position, large assembled corner parcel with protected park adjacency to the north, and transit positioning between Brightline (1.8 mi) and a planned commuter-rail station [VF/SD — SITE-16/17; S1 §6].
2. **Potential hospital-adjacent demand (unverified).** Sources cite a nearby hospital expansion (188,000 SF medical office building, 2027 opening) and a growing GME program; if verified, this **may** create complementary demand for independent providers, outpatient services, workforce support, parking, and related uses. Extent, timing, and rent implications have not been independently validated [SD — CAN-013; HC-01/02; via missing MA-07; verification MB-17]. **Required counterweight:** county medical-office vacancy was cited at 10.4% with near-term softening [SD — HC-05].
3. **Structural outpatient shift.** Care migrating off-campus; projected statewide physician-shortage figures; independent practices preferring flexible turnkey suites [SD — HC-03/04, via S5].
4. **Optionality with disciplined cost.** Convertible structure, EV conduit readiness, AV staging capability, solar/battery readiness — designed so future value layers can activate on evidence or contracts without burdening the base case [REC — CAN-030/031/033/034].

**What could make it fail** (developed in Chapter 21): rents validating at the low modeled basis with no anchor partner; the modeled yield gap persisting after program optimization; FPL capacity constraints; discretionary entitlement above six stories; seller price expectations anchored to the appraisal; construction-cost escalation; capital-markets conditions [R-13, R-2, R-4, R-5, R-16].

The thesis is an adopted strategy, not verified demand, partner support, or feasibility [CAN-010 caveat]. The models say the base program does not currently pencil (§1.3); the thesis survives only if diligence produces validated rents, a differentiated program, partner or public support, or a materially different basis.

## Part III — Demand Theses

## 7. Healthcare Demand Thesis

The demand thesis rests on three drivers, **all source-derived through a market study that is not in the repository** (MA-07); none is independently verified [SD — CAN-013]:

1. **Hospital-adjacent demand.** Cited facts: a 188,000 SF hospital medical-office building opening 2027 adjacent to the nearby campus; a 365-resident/fellow GME program described as the system's largest [SD — HC-01/02; S5 Table 19; verification MB-17]. If verified, the expansion **may** create complementary demand from independent providers, outpatient services, workforce support, and parking. The extent, timing, and rent implications have not been independently validated, and no displacement, parking-deficit, spillover-assurance, or endorsement claim is permitted [CAN-013 — blocked_pending_resolution].
2. **Parking need.** A 4.0–5.0 spaces/1,000 SF clinical planning range and a hypothesized corridor supply constraint; the existing deficit, hospital absorption, and off-peak demand are unquantified [WA — HC-11; MB-09].
3. **Outpatient shift and physician supply.** Statewide projected shortage exceeding 18,000 physician FTEs; migration of care off-campus; preference for flexible turnkey suites [SD — HC-03/04].

**Mandatory counterweight:** county medical-office vacancy was cited at **10.4%** with near-term softening [SD — HC-05]. The strategy's response is differentiation — medical co-working, parking surplus, convertibility — not generic shell space [REC — HC-09; R-3].

Site-specific tenant demand is a separate question from regional healthcare growth: **no tenant, operator, or health-system demand for this site is documented** [OQ-08; partnerships index]. Chapter 18 governs how prospective-partner conversations are represented.

## 9. Parking and Mobility Thesis

**Parking is the load-bearing mobility layer** [Canonical Mobility Strategy §1]: it satisfies the clinical parking ratio, can fund early cash flow, and is designed convertible as a hedge against long-run demand erosion [R-7]. The operating model is shared parking — medical by day, public/commuter off-peak, gateless LPR operations [REC — REV-12]. Counts are basis-dependent (Chapter 10). Revenue constructs are dual and unresolved: $2,400/space/yr blended across all spaces (S2) versus $275/stall/month on a 55% monetized share (S3) — the underlying pricing survey is not in the repository [WA — REV-04; OQ-16; MA-16; MB-09].

**EV charging.** Adopted rule: conduit and panel capacity to 100% of stalls during construction (roughly an order of magnitude cheaper than retrofit); energize a subset day-one — approximately 40 chargers as a planning placeholder [CAN-031]. The day-one mix is unresolved: 32 L2 + 8 DCFC (S2) versus 40 L2 + 4 DCFC stub-outs with hardware deferred to an operator commitment (S3) [OQ-21]. Working net income: $120,000/yr across ~40 units, utilization-sensitive (convergent) [WA — REV-05]. EV-ready totals: 80 ports (6-story) / 120 (8-story) [NRG-08]. "EV-ready" does not mean chargers installed or utility capacity confirmed [glossary].

**AV staging.** In scope now: a separated, secured ground-level staging *capability* with independent ingress/egress — capability, not operation [Current Project Scope §1]. **The governance-clean base case carries $0 of AV revenue** [CAN-012]. Upside if an operator signs (forecast class only, never base case): ~$300K/yr (S2 construct) or ~$194K/yr (S3 construct) [REV-07]. S3's current base NOI includes $194,400/yr of AV-bay revenue in tension with governance — disclosed, escalated, and corrected only in an authorized successor model [OQ-22; R-19]. Operator landscape (market context only; no partnership or site demand assumed for any of them): Waymo, Zoox, Tesla, Freebee [SD — MOB-01].

**Activation discipline.** Every Phase-3+ activation (DCFC expansion, AV build-out, level conversion) requires an explicit owner-approved demand or partner trigger; no Phase-3+ revenue enters a base-case pro forma [Current Project Scope §§4–5].
